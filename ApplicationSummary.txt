Residence Improvement Visualizer

Residence Improvement Visualizer is a Windows desktop tool for drawing a residence at true scale and showing what a proposed
change would do to it. You import a photo of a floor plan, calibrate it against one known distance,
then trace walls, doors and rooms over it. Furniture comes from a 35 item catalog with real
dimensions, and wall mounted things like grab bars snap to the face you point at. A second layer,
smart living, holds twenty five things that get installed rather than furnished. Sixteen are smart
residence sensors, costed from a Cornell Tech technology assessment, and they go on the elements they
watch, so a door sensor follows its doorway when the doorway gets widened. Nine are everyday
accessibility aids the assessment does not cover, like a rocker knife or a sock aid, which belong to
a resident or to a room rather than to a spot on the plan and which say on every screen that their
prices are ordinary retail rather than figures the assessment stands behind. Grab bars are offered
there too. Every residence keeps a locked record of how it stands today, and design options
branch off that record. Comparing two of them produces a change list in plain language with
measurements in feet and inches, and any single line can be taken back out on its own. There are
three views. Plan for tracing, an orbiting overview, and a first person walkthrough that toggles
between a standing and a seated eye height. Residents can be given a daily schedule, which places
them room by room through the day and drives a simulated run of sensor activity and caregiver
alerts. What leaves the app is a self contained HTML report, with each changed room photographed
before and after from one camera position, that prints to PDF from any browser. It runs offline.
Residences are JSON files on disk.
